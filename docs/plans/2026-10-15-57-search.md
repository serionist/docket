# Keep a query's common words, and rank a doc's title higher

## Goal

Every word of a query counts, however common, and a doc, mail or recording whose title leads with the query's words
comes first. In a vault about one customer, the customer's name narrows a search instead of leaving it.

Today `query.py` judges each form of a word, and a form in over a fifth of the chunks and over 100 leaves the query.
That fails two ways; both reproduce on the perf test's index:

| A common word whose... | Today | Invented example |
|---|---|---|
| forms are all the word itself | it leaves the query, and the CLI and the Search page say `too common to search for` | `orbex budget` searches `budget` |
| stem differs from it | its form as written is in no chunk's stemmed column, so that form counts as rare: the word stays only through forms nothing matches, the all-words stage finds nothing, and every hit is "some words", unreported | `acme budget` |

Titles count for nothing beyond their words: "Acme kick-off with Dana" ranks sixth for `kick-off acme dana`, below
chunks that only mention the words.

- In scope: a query's words (`word_groups`), the order inside the stages (titles first; in the any-word stage, the
  chunks holding more of the words), the `too common` line, a line for a word in no chunk, the recall skill's search
  advice, the tests, the perf test's shapes, the docs.
- Out of scope: the index's tables and columns (unchanged, so no rebuild); alias stem forms in mentions, which keep
  their own fifth-of-the-chunks rule (DR-0116, #60); hyphens (`kickoff` and `kick-off` stay different words, #59); a
  stopword list.

## Design

The work is in `index/query.py`; the index doesn't change. DR-0114 (columns per language) and DR-0117 (fused ranking)
stay as they are.

| Module | Change |
|---|---|
| `index/query.py` | `word_groups` keeps every form of every word; `COMMON_SHARE` and `COMMON_MIN` go; `QueryReading.left_out` stays, always empty, and `absent` lists the words in no chunk; each stage's candidates are ordered as below before they are taken |
| `render.py`, `web/views.py` | the `too common to search for` line goes; `in no chunk: <word>` comes, in the CLI, the MCP text and the Search page |
| `plugin/skills/recall/SKILL.md`, `plugin/.claude-plugin/plugin.json` | step 1 says to search with key words; the version goes to 0.2.2 (CV-0012) |
| `tests/docket_testkit.py` | `build_name_vault(root)`: an invented vault where two names are in most chunks |

### Common words

A query word is an OR of all its forms, in `folded` and in each stemmed column, and the all-words stage requires
every word. BM25 does the weighing: FTS5's IDF is low for a word in many chunks and next to nothing for one in over
half, so a common word narrows the all-words stage and barely moves the order inside it.

- It joins the match, not only the rank. As an optional term, a name in over half the chunks would get an IDF of
  almost zero and change nothing, which is the bug.
- English and Hungarian alike: every column keeps every form. Hungarian's second cut keeps its own rule (DR-0114),
  which is about meeting bare words, not about how common a form is.
- Stopwords count too: `the pallet` requires `the` in the all-words stage, as it already did in vaults of up to 100
  chunks (the rule's floor). The recall skill and the README say to search with key words (names, nouns, numbers),
  not the sentence: every word must be in a hit for it to rank first.
- In the any-word stage BM25 again gives a name in over half the chunks next to nothing, so its candidates are
  ordered by how many of the query's terms their text holds, then by BM25: for `acme budget approval`, where no chunk
  holds all three, "Acme budget" ranks above "Orbex budget".
- The `too common to search for` line goes, since no word leaves a query. `left_out` stays in the result, always
  empty, because DR-0150 keeps every field a result has.
- A word whose every form counts zero in the index's vocabulary goes into `absent`, printed as `in no chunk: <word>`,
  so the CLI and Claude see why every hit holds only some of the words. The Search page already labels such hits
  "some words".

Speed with every word kept, on the perf test's index (100,000 chunks; best of three, the maintainer's desktop):

| Query | Time |
|---|---|
| a word in 99.6% of the chunks, alone (a shape the perf test already runs) | 172 ms |
| that word and a rare one | 46 ms |
| three words each in a third to all of the chunks, and a rare one | 234 ms |
| five such words and a word in no chunk (the any-word stage) | 360 ms |
| seven such words and two words in no chunk | 484 ms |

The cost is BM25 counting each form's chunks once per query, so it grows with the number of common words. All stay
under the perf test's one second.

- Built: on CI's Windows runner, about four times slower than this desktop, "three common words and a rare one" took
  1.36 s, over the limit. Nearly all of it was the any-word stage: an OR with a word in most chunks scores every chunk.
  So the any-word and prefix stages leave out of their match a word in half the chunks or more, which FTS5's BM25
  gives no weight at all; it still counts in the any-word order through the word check, and when those stages leave
  fewer than k, one more any-word query adds the least common such word. A word's forms that no chunk holds in their
  column leave the
  expression too (they match nothing), and a term several columns hold is one phrase. After it, on this desktop: a
  common and a rare word 47 ms, three common and a rare one 140 ms, five common and a word in no chunk 188 ms, no more
  than a common word alone (188 ms, as on main). The design's 0.2 s line holds, so it stays as it was.

### Which terms a text holds

One check serves the title order and the any-word order. A text holds a term the way the index does: a word, when
one of the text's words, folded or stemmed in one of the owner's languages, is one of the word's forms; a phrase,
when its words come in that order; a name found in the query, when a title holds one of the entity's names or aliases
(the lookup that finds names in the query) or, for a chunk's text, when the chunk mentions the entity.

- Speed: stemming every word of 200 candidates of 350 words took 63 ms warm and 156 ms with a cold stem cache, as a
  fresh `docket search` has; asking FTS5 per word, restricted to the candidates' row ids, took 390 ms. So the check
  stems only the words whose first letter, folded, is one some form starts with.
- Built: the first letter, not the first three this plan first said. English's stemmer changes a word's third letter
  (`dry` to `dri`) and its exceptions the second (`dying` to `die`), so three letters missed words that stemming every
  word finds; the equivalence test caught it. A language screens only when its stemmer is known to keep a word's first
  letter (`Language.keeps_start`: English and Hungarian); with any other, every word is stemmed. Measured on 200 texts
  of 350 words and six query words: 171 ms with a cold stem cache and 41 ms warm, against 426 and 87 ms stemming every
  word.

### Titles

Within each stage's candidates, the best chunk of each group whose title's lead holds most of the query's terms goes
first.

| Chunk | Its title |
|---|---|
| a processed doc's section | the doc's title: its first `#` heading, else the slug of its id (docs have no title field) |
| a record's chunk: a mail, an event, a recording's own record | the chunk's own title (`chunks.title`): the subject, the recording's name |
| a transcript window, Plaud's notes, a thread window | the title of the record the group is named after: the recording's name, the thread parent's subject (chat threads have none) |

- A title's lead is its text up to the first `: ` (a colon and a space, so `23:44` doesn't cut it) that follows at
  least three words, else its first 12 words. A word here is a run of text between spaces holding a letter or a digit,
  so `2026-10-12` is one. A sweep note whose long heading lists everything lifts nothing with the words after its
  lead, and a label before a colon (`RE: `, `Fwd: `, `[EXT] Re: `, `Acme: `) doesn't end one:

| Title | Its lead |
|---|---|
| "2026-10-12 - Acme kick-off with Dana (second half): no site in ..." | up to "(second half)": holds all four words of `acme kick-off dana`, lifted |
| "2026-10-12 - Mail sweep, received 11 Oct 23:44 to 12 Oct 22:50: the Acme kick-off ran, ..." | up to "22:50": holds none, keeps its BM25 place |
| "RE: Granite pump overhaul", "Fwd: ...", "[EXT] Re: ...", "Acme: budget call" | the whole title: a label of one or two words ends no lead |
| "Re: Acme budget review: notes from the call" | "Re: Acme budget review" |

- "Most" is more than half of the query's words, phrases and names: both of two, two of three, three of four.
- Lifted chunks go first, ordered by the terms their lead holds, then by the share of the lead's words that are query
  terms, then by BM25. The rest follow in their stage's order. A title never lifts a chunk into an earlier stage, and
  the FTS and mention lists still fuse by rank.
- One chunk per group moves: every section of a doc carries its title on top, and moving them all would fill the top
  k with one doc.
- Titles are read at search time from `docs`, `chunks` and `records`, in one query over the candidates. The perf vault
  has no `records` rows, so a record chunk's title comes from `chunks.title`, or the perf test never runs the order.

| Considered | Why not |
|---|---|
| a title column in FTS5, weighted higher | a column more per language and a rebuild, and every window of a recording would match its name |
| a third list in the rank fusion | a title holding some of the words would lift a hit above one holding all of them |
| every titled chunk first | one long doc fills the top k |
| the whole title, however long | a sweep note's heading that lists everything is lifted for any word in it |

## Doors and data

None. The MCP tools keep their names and parameters, and the result keeps every field (DR-0150): `left_out` stays,
always empty, and `absent` is new. The index's schema and columns don't change, so no index rebuilds, and nothing in
the vault is written.
