# Rerank the fused top 50 with a cross-encoder in the worker

With models installed, search sends the query and its top candidates to a cross-encoder in #67's worker and orders
them again, all-words hits first. On the invented set that takes paraphrase recall at 10 from 0.44 to 0.78 on a GPU and
to 0.61 on a CPU. Past a 0.75 s budget, with no worker, or with a reranker that can't run, the order stays what it is
today, vectors stay on, and the result says why.

## Goal

| Part | Done when |
|---|---|
| Models | bge-reranker-v2-m3 (fp16 ONNX) on CUDA, mmarco-mMiniLMv2-L12-H384-v1 (fp32 ONNX) on the CPU, pinned by revision and sha256 in the catalogue, licences recorded |
| Worker | a `rerank` op beside `embed` in #67's worker; the service keeps both models loaded; a reranker that can't load or runs on the wrong provider is off, and the embedder keeps serving |
| Search | today's top k, then the weight-1 order, up to 50 on a GPU and 30 on a CPU, ordered in two tiers within a 0.75 s budget; skipped, the result keeps today's order and says why |
| Install | `docket models install` fetches the device's reranker and times it; `--no-reranker` leaves it out; `status` and `remove` know it |
| Measured | the question set with the CPU reranker against floors of its own in the models CI job; the GPU numbers in the PR; the perf limits hold |

- In scope: the catalogue, the worker, the client, the provider, the install, the config's `reranker` key, the rerank
  step in search, `SearchResult.rerank`, the CLI and eval lines, the models job, the docs.
- Out of scope: the CUDA pins on Windows (#109, merged first); `docket search` through the running service when it
  serves the vault (#110, a follow-up); the API provider (#89); int8 or other model files; MPS or CoreML on Macs; the
  Settings page (#73).

## Design

### The models

bge-reranker-v2-m3 on CUDA, as the issue says; mmarco-mMiniLMv2-L12-H384-v1 in fp32 on the CPU (DR-0450).

- mmarco is the only multilingual candidate that reranks 30 pairs in under a second on 4 threads. gte orders better
  (all kinds 0.90 / 0.77) at four times the cost in fp32 and twice in int8; bge in int8 orders best of all on a CPU
  (0.93 / 0.81) at four times the cost.
- fp32, not int8: the hub has one int8 file per instruction set, and int8 kernels differ by CPU, so one search would
  rank differently on the desktop, on CI's runner and on a Mac. int8 is 1.6 times faster on 4 threads.
- Licences and lineage:
  - mmarco is Apache-2.0. Its base, nreimers/mMiniLMv2-L12-H384-distilled-from-XLMR-Large, has no licence on its card;
    it is Microsoft's MiniLMv2 (MIT) distilled from XLM-R Large (MIT), uploaded by the same author.
  - bge-reranker-v2-m3 is Apache-2.0. The GPU file is onnx-community's automatic conversion, with no licence file of
    its own, as #67's notes say.
  - Both are trained on MS MARCO: mmarco's card names mMARCO (Apache-2.0), a machine translation of it, and bge's
    training data (bge-m3-data) lists it. MS MARCO's own terms are for non-commercial research. The models' Apache-2.0
    governs their use, and Docket never ships either file: the user's install fetches it.

### The ranking contract: two tiers

The reranker orders the top N in two tiers: the chunks the all-words stage found, then every other candidate of the top
N (DR-0452). Within each tier, chunks lifted by an explicit people or entity boost (the search's `people` and
`entities`) come first, then the rest, each part by the reranker's score. That keeps DR-0338's rule, a chunk found only
by meaning never passes an all-words hit, and goes one step further: any-word, prefix and entity hits stay below them
too. A boost keeps what it means: it lifts a chunk, and the reranker doesn't undo that.

| Contract | GPU: exact-name, paraphrase, inflection, time-bound, all | CPU (top 30): the same |
|---|---|---|
| two tiers | 1.00/0.84, 0.78/0.58, 0.96, 0.95, 0.94/0.82 | 1.00/0.76, 0.67/0.35, 0.96, 0.81, 0.90/0.70 |
| override: the reranker's order alone | 1.00/0.84, 0.78/0.58, 0.92, 0.95, 0.94/0.82 | 0.93/0.74, 0.67/0.35, 0.87, 0.81, 0.89/0.67 |
| obey: rerank freely, then DR-0338's rule again | same as two tiers | 0.93/0.74, 0.67/0.35, 0.96, 0.81, 0.89/0.69 |
| two tiers, all-words tier left in keyword order | 1.00/0.86, 0.78/0.58, 0.92, 0.82, 0.94/0.80 | 1.00/0.86, 0.67/0.35, 0.92, 0.72, 0.90/0.69 |
| the fused rank and the reranker's fused by RRF (#67's CPU direction) | 1.00/0.88, 0.78/0.45, 0.89, 0.80, 0.94/0.74 | 1.00/0.82, 0.67/0.32, 0.85, 0.77, 0.92/0.67 |

Measured with the top N from weight 1; no question in the set uses a boost.

- Paraphrase is the same under every contract but RRF: its answers are seldom all-words hits, so the tiers cost it
  nothing. Exact names are what the tiers protect: overriding lost one on the CPU, to an any-word hit.
- Inside the all-words tier the reranker's order replaces the title-lead order (DR-0231, DR-0232) for that search:
  exact-name MRR falls from 0.88 to 0.84 on the GPU and 0.76 on the CPU, recall stays 1.00. Keeping the keyword order
  there cost more in time-bound than it saved in exact names.

### The top N

The top N starts with today's top k, then fills by walking the weight-1 order from its top, skipping chunks already in,
until it holds N; past the top N the order is today's (DR-0453). Today's top k is the chunks behind today's k hits:
today's order (weight 0.5, what the search returns without a reranker) walked with the search's own collapsing until it
has kept k hits, the k hits' own chunks first, then the chunks folded into their `also`. So every one of today's hits
is scored whenever k is N or less, as at the MCP tool's k 20 on a CPU's 30 pairs over threads whose hits fold many
chunks. The reranker can reorder today's hits but never drops one unseen. When those chunks are N or more, the top N is
today's alone. DR-0338's move runs again on the final order, a guard for a chunk only the vectors found that sits in the
top N, as one folded into a hit can, above an all-words hit left outside it.

| Top N from | GPU paraphrase | CPU paraphrase | Order when skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| today's top k, then weight 1 (chosen) | 0.78 / 0.58 | 0.61 / 0.32 | today's |
| weight 1 alone | 0.78 / 0.58 | 0.67 / 0.35 | weight 1 alone would cost inflection MRR 0.86 to 0.80, time-bound 0.78 to 0.70 |
| weight 0.5 alone | 0.72 / 0.53 | 0.61 / 0.29 | today's |

The eval runs at k 10. Filling from weight 1 after today's top 10 kept every GPU number; on the CPU it cost one
paraphrase, whose answer weight 1 alone brought into the 30. Measured with today's first 10 chunks before collapsing;
the build measures with the collapsed top k, and the floors take what it gives. Fusing at both weights costs nothing next to the rerank.
A skipped rerank gives exactly today's result, so `eval-floors-models.json` keeps holding it.

### Pairs, the budget, and what the result says

- Pairs: 50 on a GPU, 30 on a CPU. The install times them on invented pairs of real length, the median of three after a
  warm-up, with the same session settings searches use, so a hybrid CPU's efficiency cores count as they will in a
  search; the reranker is on only within 0.6 s (DR-0455). The RTX 5090 needs 0.13 s; the desktop's CPU 0.42 s on its 16
  cores and 0.56 s on 8, so 8 cores qualify and 4 (0.87 s) or 2 (1.32 s) don't: a 4-vCPU runner reranks with 30 pairs
  only in the floors test, which forces them.
- No step down to 20 pairs: on the set they gained MRR only (all kinds 0.86 / 0.69 against 0.86 / 0.65) and lost the
  same typo question as 30.
- Budget: 0.75 s from when the search asks (DR-0454). The request carries the deadline as a wall-clock time and the
  worker knows the install's time for a batch of 10, so it starts no batch, the first one included, that it expects to
  end past the deadline; the client stops waiting at the deadline plus 0.2 s. A pass batch in the worker's hands
  (about 0.3 s, DR-0330) counts against the budget.
- After any query passes its 2 s on a worker, the worker counts as busy until it answers anything, and a rerank skips
  without asking ("the models' worker is busy: a query passed its time"): a request would only queue behind it.
- After three budget skips in a row the search stops asking for five minutes and says so, then asks again. The
  provider counts reranks done and skipped for the budget; `docket models status`, the service's status and
  `/api/models` show both.
- Skipped (past the budget, paused, worker down, reranker off in the worker, an error, nothing to compare when the query
  is only time words or filters): today's order, and `SearchResult.rerank` says why (DR-0459). The CLI, the MCP text and
  the Search page add one line only when the reranker is on and was skipped, as for the vector stage.
- `docket eval` and the floors test rerank with no budget (a 30 s wait a question), so their numbers don't move with the
  machine's load; eval prints the reranker and its pairs above its table and how many questions went without it.

```python
class RerankRun(BaseModel):
    state: Literal["off", "on", "skipped"] = "off"  # off: no reranker here, or off on this machine
    model: str | None = None
    pairs: int = 0
    why: str | None = None
```

### The worker

A second ONNX Runtime session in the same worker, and an op beside `embed` (DR-0451).

- The spec names the reranker only when it is on for this machine: its model and tokenizer files, its token limit, its
  device and the install's time for a batch of 10. The floors test passes a spec of its own that names it, whatever
  the runner's timing decided.
- The worker loads it after the embedder and runs one pair before its hello. A reranker that fails to load, or whose
  session gets a provider other than its device's (a CUDA session that fell back to the CPU would take about 4.3 s for
  10 pairs), is off in that worker: the hello says so and why, `docket models status` shows it, and the embedder keeps
  serving. A failure to load the reranker never ends the worker, as a failure to load the embedder does today.
- `{"id": n, "op": "rerank", "query": "...", "texts": [...], "deadline": <unix seconds>}` answers
  `{"id": n, "ok": true, "scores": "<base64 float32>"}`, one score per text, or `{"ok": false, "error": "PastBudget"}`,
  or `RerankerOff` when it is off in the worker; every error is a type name only (DR-0155), and no text is ever logged.
- Each pair is the query and a chunk's input without the passage prefix (its title on a line of its own unless its
  opening holds it, then its text), capped at 4,096 characters by the core and truncated longest-first by the tokenizer,
  so a long query never fails a pair. The query is the vector stage's: words and phrases without time words.
- Batches of 10 pairs on both devices: the same time as one batch on the GPU with half the VRAM, faster on the CPU, and
  a point to check the deadline.
- The CPU session runs on ONNX Runtime's default threads, one per physical core. The pass's `threads()`, half the
  logical cores, is 16 on the 32-thread desktop too; the two differ on a CPU without SMT, where half the cores would
  halve a rerank's threads (865 ms on 4 against 563 on 8 here), and on a hybrid CPU whose efficiency cores run one
  thread each. The CUDA session's arena grows by what it asks for.
- The client counts a rerank as a query, so the pass's batches wait behind it.

### The install, status and remove

| | CPU | CUDA |
|---|---|---|
| reranker | mmarco-minilm, 0.49 GB with its tokenizer | bge-reranker-v2-m3, 1.15 GB |
| model files in all, with e5-small | 0.98 GB | 1.64 GB |
| new packages | none: the environments hold ONNX Runtime and tokenizers already | none |

- `docket models install` fetches the device's reranker with the embedder; `--no-reranker` writes `reranker: none`,
  fetches nothing for it and removes its files if an earlier install fetched them (DR-0456). One reranker per device.
- It checks the reranker's provider on CUDA, times its pairs and writes into the environment's marker, beside the batch
  budget, the reranker's name and revision with its pairs and time. A marker whose name or revision isn't the
  catalogue's reads as untimed: the reranker is off until the install times it again. A marker #67 wrote has no
  reranker fields at all: it still reads, the embedder stays on, and the reranker is untimed. Off on this machine, the
  install says the time and that `--no-reranker` frees the files.
- The config's `models:` block gains `reranker`. A block written by #67 has none: no reranker, and `docket models
  status` says the install adds it. An unknown reranker name reads as the reranker off, with the reason; the embedder
  stays on (DR-0335, DR-0451). Any other key or value that doesn't read still turns models off.
- `docket models status`: `reranker  mmarco-minilm (Apache-2.0) on cpu: 30 pairs in 0.42 s; 120 reranked, 3 past the
  budget` or `off on this machine: 30 pairs took 0.87 s, past 0.6 s; docket models install times it again`; `--check`
  scores a pair. `/api/models` carries the same. `docket models remove` removes the reranker's files with the rest.

### The perf test

- In the gate, model-free: every shape of `test_index_perf.py` runs a third time, with the vector stage and an
  in-process fake reranker that answers at once, under the same 1 s. That times the core's share: two fusions, reading
  the top 50's text by id, the tiers.
- A fake worker whose rerank sleeps past the budget: the search answers within its keyword time plus 0.95 s, in
  today's order, saying why, and the next query's vector still comes within its 2 s because the worker stopped itself.
  Both carry `@pytest.mark.perf` (DR-0430).
- On a real machine a rerank adds what the install timed: 0.13 s on the GPU here, 0.42 s on the desktop's CPU. On a
  CPU that qualifies with little room (8 cores, 0.56 s) a search over 100,000 chunks can take about 1 s.

### CI

- The models job's install fetches the CPU reranker too. Its files join the Actions cache under the same key, which
  hashes the catalogue. An entry holds 0.678 GB with both models, against 0.281 GB with the embedder (measured on #117),
  about 2 GB across the three systems, and as much again for a PR that changes the catalogue until it merges. The first
  run after the change downloads 0.49 GB more on each system.
- The repo's cache is past its 10 GB (10.67 GB when #117 was reviewed). Past the limit GitHub saves the new entry and
  evicts the least recently used until the total fits: old PRs' setup-uv and model entries go first, and main's model
  entries, restored by every models job, stay. A branch whose entry was evicted downloads the files from the hub again,
  a minute more and an outage's risk.
- `gate models` runs the set twice on Linux: with the embedder alone against `eval-floors-models.json`, unchanged, and
  with the reranker at 30 pairs and no budget against a new `eval-floors-rerank.json`, whatever the runner's own timing
  decided (DR-0458). The new file starts with `"$above": "print"`, as #100's did, and turns to `"fail"` once three CI
  runs give identical numbers. The lane prints the runner's CPU name beside the rerank numbers and the install's
  timing. The reranked run adds about a minute: the set took 38 s on 2 of the desktop's threads.
- Windows and macOS install both models, and the install's timing is what runs the real reranker there: their
  runners time it off (2.81 s and 9.71 s for 30 pairs on #117), so `docket models status --check` starts their worker
  without it. The step is named "The question set with the embedder and the reranker" on Linux.
- The gate stays model-free: the fake worker gains a `rerank` that scores by shared trigrams, so a test can plant an
  order.
- The GPU numbers come from the maintainer's desktop and go in the PR. CI has no GPU, so nothing there fetches the
  1.14 GB file: its pin is checked by the maintainer's install.
