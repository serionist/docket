# Rerank the fused top 50 with a cross-encoder in the worker

With models installed, search sends the query and its fused top candidates to a cross-encoder in #67's worker and
orders them again, all-words hits first. On the invented set that takes paraphrase recall at 10 from 0.44 to 0.78 on a
GPU and to 0.67 on a CPU. Past a 0.75 s budget, or with no worker, the order stays what it is today and the result
says why.

## Goal

| Part | Done when |
|---|---|
| Models | bge-reranker-v2-m3 (fp16 ONNX) on CUDA, mmarco-mMiniLMv2-L12-H384-v1 (fp32 ONNX) on the CPU, pinned by revision and sha256 in the catalogue, licences recorded |
| Worker | a `rerank` op beside `embed` in #67's worker; the service keeps both models loaded |
| Search | the top 50 on a GPU, 30 on a CPU, ordered in two tiers within a 0.75 s budget; skipped, the result keeps today's order and says why |
| Install | `docket models install` fetches the device's reranker and times it; `--no-reranker` leaves it out; `status` and `remove` know it |
| Measured | the question set with the CPU reranker against floors of its own in the models CI job; the GPU numbers in the PR; the perf limits hold |

- In scope: the catalogue, the worker, the client, the provider, the install, the config's `reranker` key, the rerank
  step in search, `SearchResult.rerank`, the CLI and eval lines, the models job, the docs.
- Out of scope: the API provider (#89); `docket search` through the running service (named under Who reranks);
  int8 or other model files; MPS or CoreML on Macs; the Settings page (#73).

## Design

### The models

bge-reranker-v2-m3 on CUDA, as the issue says; mmarco-mMiniLMv2-L12-H384-v1 in fp32 on the CPU (DR-0450).

- mmarco is the only multilingual candidate that reranks 30 pairs in under a second on 4 threads. gte orders better
  (all kinds 0.90 / 0.77) at four times the cost in fp32 and twice in int8; bge in int8 orders best of all on a CPU
  (0.93 / 0.81) at four times the cost.
- fp32, not int8: the hub has one int8 file per instruction set, and int8 kernels differ by CPU, so one search would
  rank differently on the desktop, on CI's runner and on a Mac. int8 is 1.6 times faster on 4 threads.
- Its card names its training data: mMARCO (Apache-2.0), a machine translation of MS MARCO, whose own terms are for
  non-commercial research. The model's Apache-2.0 governs its use; it is said here and in the entry for the lead.
- The GPU file is onnx-community's automatic conversion of BAAI's Apache-2.0 model, with no licence file of its own,
  as #67's notes say. Docket never ships either file: the user's install fetches it.

### The ranking contract: two tiers

The reranker orders the top N in two tiers: the chunks the all-words stage found, by score, then every other candidate
of the top N, by score (DR-0452). That keeps DR-0338's rule, a chunk found only by meaning never passes an all-words
hit, and goes one step further: any-word, prefix and entity hits stay below them too.

| Contract | GPU: exact-name, paraphrase, inflection, time-bound, all | CPU (top 30): the same |
|---|---|---|
| two tiers | 1.00/0.84, 0.78/0.58, 0.96, 0.95, 0.94/0.82 | 1.00/0.76, 0.67/0.35, 0.96, 0.81, 0.90/0.70 |
| override: the reranker's order alone | 1.00/0.84, 0.78/0.58, 0.92, 0.95, 0.94/0.82 | 0.93/0.74, 0.67/0.35, 0.87, 0.81, 0.89/0.67 |
| obey: rerank freely, then DR-0338's rule again | same as two tiers | 0.93/0.74, 0.67/0.35, 0.96, 0.81, 0.89/0.69 |
| two tiers, all-words tier left in keyword order | 1.00/0.86, 0.78/0.58, 0.92, 0.82, 0.94/0.80 | 1.00/0.86, 0.67/0.35, 0.92, 0.72, 0.90/0.69 |
| the fused rank and the reranker's fused by RRF (#67's CPU direction) | 1.00/0.88, 0.78/0.45, 0.89, 0.80, 0.94/0.74 | 1.00/0.82, 0.67/0.32, 0.85, 0.77, 0.92/0.67 |

- Paraphrase is the same under every contract but RRF: its answers are seldom all-words hits, so the tiers cost it
  nothing. Exact names are what the tiers protect: overriding lost one on the CPU, to an any-word hit.
- Inside the all-words tier the reranker's order replaces the title-lead order (DR-0231, DR-0232) for that search: exact-name
  MRR falls from 0.88 to 0.84 on the GPU and 0.76 on the CPU, recall stays 1.00. Keeping the keyword order there cost
  more in time-bound than it saved in exact names.
- A boost lifts a chunk into the top N, and the reranker then orders it within its tier.

### The fusion's weight

A search that reranks fuses twice, which costs nothing next to the rerank (DR-0453): at vector weight 1 for the
reranker's top N, which fed it better, and at 0.5 (DR-0338) for everything past the reranked top and for the whole
order when the rerank is skipped.

| Candidates from | GPU paraphrase | CPU paraphrase | Order when skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| weight 1 | 0.78 / 0.58 | 0.67 / 0.35 | weight 1 alone: inflection MRR 0.80, time-bound 0.70 |
| weight 0.5 | 0.72 / 0.53 | 0.61 / 0.29 | weight 0.5: today's 0.86 and 0.78 |

So a skipped rerank gives exactly today's result, and `eval-floors-models.json` keeps holding it.

### Pairs, the budget, and what the result says

- Pairs: 50 on a GPU, 30 on a CPU. The install times them on invented pairs of real length, the median of three after a
  warm-up, and the reranker is on only within 0.6 s (DR-0455). The RTX 5090 needs 0.13 s; the desktop's CPU 0.42 s on
  16 cores and 0.56 s on 8, so 8 cores qualify and 4 (0.87 s) or 2 (1.32 s) don't: a 4-vCPU runner reranks with 30
  pairs only in the floors test, which forces them.
- No step down to 20 pairs: on the set they gained MRR only (all kinds 0.86 / 0.69 against 0.86 / 0.65) and lost the
  same typo question as 30.
- Budget: 0.75 s from when the search asks (DR-0454). The request carries the deadline as a wall-clock time; the worker
  stops between batches of 10 when the next would end past it, and the client stops waiting at the deadline plus 0.2 s.
  A pass batch in the worker's hands (about 0.3 s, DR-0330) counts against the budget.
- Skipped (past the budget, worker down, an error, nothing to compare when the query is only time words or filters):
  the weight-0.5 order, as today, and `SearchResult.rerank` says why (DR-0459). The CLI, the MCP text and the Search
  page add one line only when the reranker is on and was skipped, as for the vector stage.
- `docket eval` and the floors test rerank with no budget (a 30 s wait a question), so their numbers don't move with the
  machine's load; eval prints the reranker and its pairs above its table and how many questions went without it.

```python
class RerankRun(BaseModel):
    state: Literal["off", "on", "skipped"] = "off"  # off: no reranker here, or off on this machine
    model: str | None = None
    pairs: int = 0
    why: str | None = None
```

### The worker

A second ONNX Runtime session in the same worker, and an op beside `embed` (DR-0451).

- The spec gains the reranker's model and tokenizer files, its token limit and its device. The worker loads it after the
  embedder and runs one pair before its hello, which names the reranker and the provider its session got, so the install
  sees a CUDA session that fell back to the CPU.
- `{"id": n, "op": "rerank", "query": "...", "texts": [...], "deadline": <unix seconds>}` answers
  `{"id": n, "ok": true, "scores": "<base64 float32>"}`, one score per text, or `{"ok": false, "error": "PastBudget"}`;
  every error is a type name only (DR-0155), and no text is ever logged.
- Each pair is the query and a chunk's input without the passage prefix (its title on a line of its own unless its
  opening holds it, then its text), capped at 4,096 characters by the core and truncated longest-first by the tokenizer,
  so a long query never fails a pair. The query is the vector stage's: words and phrases without time words.
- Batches of 10 pairs on both devices: the same time as one batch on the GPU with half the VRAM, faster on the CPU, and
  a point to check the deadline.
- The CPU session runs on ONNX Runtime's default threads, one per physical core: a rerank is short and someone waits on
  it, where the pass keeps to half the cores. The CUDA session's arena grows by what it asks for.
- The client counts a rerank as a query, so the pass's batches wait behind it.

- `docket models install` fetches the device's reranker with the embedder; `--no-reranker` writes `reranker: none`,
  fetches nothing for it and removes its files if an earlier install fetched them (DR-0456). One reranker per device.
- It checks the reranker's provider on CUDA, times its pairs and writes `rerank_pairs` and `rerank_seconds` into the
  environment's marker, beside the batch budget. Off on this machine, it says the time and that `--no-reranker` frees
  the files.
- The config's `models:` block gains `reranker`. A block written by #67 has none: no reranker, and `docket models
  status` says the install adds it. An unknown name turns models off with the reason, as any value that doesn't read
  does (DR-0335).
- `docket models status`: `reranker  mmarco-minilm (Apache-2.0) on cpu: 30 pairs in 0.42 s` or `off on this machine:
  30 pairs took 0.87 s, past 0.6 s`; `--check` scores a pair. `/api/models` carries the same. `docket models remove`
  removes the reranker's files with the rest.

### The perf test

- In the gate, model-free: every shape of `test_index_perf.py` runs a third time, with the vector stage and an
  in-process fake reranker that answers at once, under the same 1 s. That times the core's share: two fusions, reading
  the top 50's text by id, the tiers.
- A fake worker whose rerank sleeps past the budget: the search answers within its keyword time plus 0.95 s, in
  today's order, saying why, and the next query's vector still comes within its 2 s because the worker stopped itself.
  Both carry `@pytest.mark.perf` (DR-0430).
- On a real machine a rerank adds what the install timed: 0.13 s on the GPU here, 0.42 s on the desktop's CPU. On a
  CPU that qualifies with little room (8 cores, 0.56 s) a search over 100,000 chunks can take about 1 s.

### CI

- The models job's install fetches the CPU reranker too. Its files join the Actions cache under the same key, which
  hashes the catalogue: the first run after the change downloads 0.49 GB more on each system, and each restore after
  carries about 0.98 GB instead of 0.49, some 3 GB across the three systems.
- `gate models` runs the set twice on Linux: with the embedder alone against `eval-floors-models.json`, unchanged, and
  with the reranker at 30 pairs and no budget against a new `eval-floors-rerank.json`, whatever the runner's own timing
  decided (DR-0458). Above-floor follows DR-0339: printed until three CI runs agree, then failing. The reranked run adds
  about a minute: the set took 38 s on 2 of the desktop's threads.
- Windows and macOS install both models, and `docket models status --check` scores a pair.
- The gate stays model-free: the fake worker gains a `rerank` that scores by shared trigrams, so a test can plant an
  order.
- The GPU numbers come from the maintainer's desktop and go in the PR. CI has no GPU, so nothing there fetches the
  1.14 GB file: its pin is checked by the maintainer's install.

## Doors and data

No one-way door changes:

- the MCP tools (DR-0150): `search` keeps its parameters; its result gains `rerank`, additive, and its hits keep their
  fields and `found_by` values;
- `vault.yaml` (DR-0021), the question file (DR-0290), the plugin's names, the vault's format: untouched;
- the index: unchanged, no table added; the reranker reads chunks' text through the search's own connection.

Not doors, but contracts that change: the user config's `models:` block gains `reranker` (not a door before the first
release, DR-0335); the environment's marker gains `rerank_pairs` and `rerank_seconds`, and an older marker reads as no
reranker until the install runs again; the worker protocol gains `rerank` (the core and the worker ship together).
Stored data: none; the reranker's files live in the user's cache folder beside the embedder's.

Named for later: the API provider (#89) would send the query and the top 50 chunks' text off the machine on every
search; its opt-in is a `vault.yaml` key, a DR-0021 door.
