# Keep entities and aliases up in three tiers, with the curate skill and page

## Goal

Names stay findable in every spelling for a few minutes of review a week: what a system states is applied by itself,
the rest comes as a ranked weekly batch the user decides in chat or on the Curate page, and every change can be undone.

| Part | The issue's "done when" | This plan |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | facts from a system and a name two engines spell the same, applied by themselves, logged and reversible | four rules (an address, a chat id, a name a system shows for a known id, a spelling two engines agree on), on the primary machine at a round's start; never a new entity |
| Tier 2 | at most about 15 a week, ranked by how much the gap hurts search, each with a count, snippets and locators; a rejection on a stoplist | at most 15 cards, ranked by the chunks search misses today; snippets cut from the index when shown; a rejection stoplists the name and what only joined it |
| Skill | `curate` runs a round in chat | `/docket:curate` over `docket curate`, with Claude's call per card, and seeding from a people directory |
| Page | the same batch, with accept, edit and reject | `/curate`: the batch, what tier 1 applied with Undo, the log with Undo and Lift |
| Measured | review minutes per week on the dashboard; the question set before and after a round | minutes estimated from the log, on the Curate page and in the session-start status; `docket eval` on the user's own set at a round's start and end |

The cold start decides most of this. The maintainer's vault holds no entities and no mentions: about 260 Plaud
recordings in Hungarian and English and about 200 old notes. Plaud gives tier 1 nothing (no addresses, no ids, one
transcript per recording), so the first rounds are tier 2 cards, named from speaker labels and the old notes and
ranked by how the transcripts and Plaud's own text mishear them; plus seeding from a file the user already keeps.

- In scope: the store (entity writer, curate log, stoplist, undo); the miner and the batch; tier 1's rules; `docket
  curate`; the plugin's `curate` skill; the Curate page; the session-start line; both measures.
- Out of scope:
  - the MCP tools `propose` and `entity` from the design's table: their own issue; proposals from other skills then
    land in the same log;
  - merging two entities, removing an alias, or editing `needs` from a card: by hand in the file;
  - phonetic spellings across languages beyond the edit distance (an English name written by its Hungarian sound):
    the skill can spot them, the miner doesn't;
  - a scheduler: a round starts when asked; Today and scheduled jobs (phase 3) can start one later;
  - corrections learned from reconciling two transcripts (phase 2, the design's Speech section);
  - any round on the maintainer's vault by an agent: he runs his first round himself after PR 2 merges.

## Design

### The tiers

The issue names three tiers and describes two. The design's Keeping it up section gives the third: never shown,
one-off capitalised words and rare n-grams, which the miner drops before anything is ranked. It also has a seed step
before the tiers.

| Tier | What | Applied | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| seed | entities the user already keeps: a people directory, a glossary | on the user's yes to a changes file, `docket curate add` | the skill drafts the file from what the user points at |
| 1 | facts a system states; a spelling two engines agree on | by itself, logged, listed at the next round with Undo | records' authors; two engines' transcripts |
| 2 | everything else worth a look | by the user in a round: accept, edit, reject | the miner, over the index |
| 3 | one-off capitalised words, rare phrases, common words, never-store matches, stoplisted spellings | never: the miner's filter, its counts in one line | the miner |

### Where proposals come from

The miner reads the index only (chunks, segments, records, docs, entities, aliases, `alias_terms`, mentions, the
vocabulary), never the vault's files, so it costs no model and gives the same cards on any machine with the same index
(DR-0466). A spelling's place sets its trust, highest first:

| Trust | Spelling | Where | Counts as |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | label | a transcript's speaker label, not a generic one (`Speaker 1`, `Beszélő 2`, `Unknown`): the user typed it in the recorder's app | written |
| 2 | docs | the user's processed docs: old notes, minutes, notes | written |
| 3 | mail and chat | records people typed, a mail's subject included | written |
| 4 | vendor text | a vendor summary, the title of a recording or a transcript: a vendor's model wrote it from what it heard | heard |
| 5 | transcript | transcript text, each line's label prefix left out | heard |

A card's name is the run a single word joined, when there is one (`Cleo Test` over the label `Cleo`); between
spellings of the same shape, the most trusted, ties broken by containers. Only a fuzzy join needs a written anchor;
case and `'s` forms join their base whatever its trust.

1. A spelling is a capitalised word or a run of two or three, an all-caps word of 2 to 6 letters, or a mixed-case
   word (`DevOps`). A single word counts only away from a sentence's start (a chunk's or a line's start, after `.`,
   `!` or `?`); a run counts wherever it starts, so `Participants: Ada Example, Ben Sample` gives both. A spelling
   that qualifies is then counted everywhere it is, sentence starts included.
2. Tier 3 leaves out (DR-0465):
   - a spelling in one container only; a run seen under three times;
   - a common word, written lower case in over a fifth of its mid-sentence uses: alone (`Okay`, `Szóval`), as a run's
     first word at a sentence's start (`Szóval Ada` keeps `Ada`), or as every word of a run (`Next Week`);
   - a language's time words and names (`October`, `Hungarian`), and the words of generic labels (`Speaker`);
   - under three letters, or digits only;
   - a spelling an alias covers: its written form or an `asr` misspelling in `alias_terms`, never a case form or a stem
     form, so `Anna` stays a candidate beside an entity Ann whose `Anná` folds to it;
   - a spelling a never-store list matches, counted without its value;
   - a key on the stoplist, in the role it names.

   Its counts show as one line: "left out: 412 one-off words, 38 rare phrases, 96 common words, 2 on your never-store
   lists, 7 rejected".
3. Spellings group, in both languages:
   - folded the same: `Dóra`, `Dora`;
   - two words seen in both orders are one name: `Dóra Minta`, `Minta Dóra`;
   - a heard spelling within matching's fuzzy distance (none under 5 letters, one edit for 5 to 7, two from 8) of a
     written spelling, or a Hungarian case form (`Language.cases`, as matching reads them) or an English `'s` form of
     any spelling, written or heard, joins it only when the two meet in at least two recordings, a recording counting
     with the docs that cover it (`doc_covers`): `Nortwin` with `Northwind`, `Dórával` with `Dóra`;
   - a case form never joins when it is itself a label or the head of a written run of its own: Hungarian's case forms
     of Ann, Jane, Jess, Éva and Leo fold to `Anna`, `Janet`, `Jesse`, `Evan` and `Leon`, which a label or `Anna
     Sample` in a note keeps as their own names;
   - a single word whose recordings are mostly a run's joins it as a plain alias: `Cleo` in `Cleo Test`'s.
4. Entities first: a group that reaches an entity's name or alias the same ways, seen where that entity is mentioned,
   is an alias card for it, made only when accepting it would add a mention; a label whose first word is one
   person's name or alias is too. The rest are new-entity cards.
5. A new card's guesses, all editable: a person when a label or an author backs it, or both name orders are seen,
   else a term; the id the name's slug; the name under the owner's first language and the other order a plain alias.
   The miner doesn't tell a text's language: the edit moves the other order under `hu`.

On the maintainer's vault the first batch is new people and terms: labels where he renamed speakers in Plaud and names
in his old notes give the names; Plaud's titles, summaries and transcripts give the misheard forms and the gaps that
rank them.

### How much the gap hurts search

A card's rank is the chunks a search for the name misses today that the change would find (DR-0467).

| Card | Missed |
|---|---|
| alias for an entity | chunks holding the spelling that don't mention the entity (`mentions`): what accepting adds |
| new entity | chunks holding any of its spellings that the FTS expression search builds for its name doesn't match (`query.py`'s word groups, made public) |

- Ranked by missed containers (recordings, threads, docs), then missed chunks, then the newest: a name misheard in nine
  recordings beats one misheard nine times in one.
- A name always spelled the same and always found ranks below every name with a gap, by its containers, so a quiet
  week still has cards.
- The count reads "missed in 11 chunks of 5 recordings, newest 14 Oct".

### Snippets and locators

Up to three per card, cut from the index's chunk text when the card is shown and never stored (DR-0468): one per
container, newest first; the first where the name is written too (the evidence the two are one), then where the
spelling stands alone (what search misses). About 160 characters around the spelling, which is marked, with the
chunk's locator (`r:<rid>@mm:ss`, `<doc>#L4`) linked to the Search page's panel. A forgotten record's words never
outlive it in a batch.

### A round

| Step | What |
|---|---|
| start | the page's button, `docket curate round`, or the skill. In order: the index brought up to date; tier 1's commit on the primary machine; the index updated again, so the miner sees it; the miner; the batch written |
| threads | in the service only tier 1's commit and the batch's write run on the writer thread; the miner reads the index beside it |
| batch | at most 15 cards, one per entity or new name, numbered, in the vault's private folder as `curate/batch.json`: per machine, disposable, no door |
| decide | accept, edit (accept with changes) or reject, naming the card by its id, or by the round and its number. A decision for a round that isn't this machine's current one is refused with the current round's id, so a number read off an old list never decides another card |
| state | folded from the log by the card's keys: the page, the CLI and the skill on one machine show one batch, and a decision synced from another machine closes the card here; a card whose change a hand edit already made shows as done |
| due | 7 days after the last round on any machine, read from the log; another round starts whenever asked, so a cold start can run several in a sitting |

### The log, the stoplist and undo

Every change Docket makes to entities, by tier 1 or the user, and every rejection, is a line of
`entities/_log/<yyyy-mm>-<machine>.jsonl`, committed with the change (DR-0460).

```json
{"schema_version": 1, "id": "20261016T091402-desk-1", "op": "add", "at": "2026-10-16T09:14:02Z", "machine": "desk",
 "by": "user", "via": "page", "round": "2026-10-16T0912-desk", "card": "3f2a1c", "key": "alias:northwind:nortwin",
 "entity": "northwind", "change": {"alias": {"text": "Nortwin", "kind": "asr"}},
 "evidence": {"chunks": 9, "containers": 4, "locators": ["r:plaud:rec-0001/transaction@12:08"]}}
```

| Op | What |
|---|---|
| `create` | a new entity, its fields as written |
| `add` | an alias, a name, an id or a related link added to one |
| `reject` | one stoplisted key; a card's rejection is a line per key |
| `undo` | the inverse of one op, named by `of`, applied to the file as it stands |
| `lift` | a key taken off the stoplist |

- `by` is user or tier1; `via` is cli or page (the skill's are cli).
- A key is `<kind>:<entity or ->:<text>` (DR-0461), the text as `aliases.folded` holds it: the index's `fold` over
  `words()`, joined by one space; an id's value casefolded. `new:-:nortwin` says a spelling isn't a new entity's
  name; `alias:northwind:nortwin` that it isn't that entity's alias; `id:ada-example:email=ada@example.com`,
  `name:dora-minta:hu=minta dora` and `related:northwind:person=ben-sample` name what an add added.
- Tier 1 applies a key at most once: a fact the user undid or deleted by hand stays gone.
- Per machine and month, as the ledger's ops; the vault's `*.jsonl merge=union` already covers it, and the entity
  loader already skips `_log/`. Read leniently: a line that doesn't read is reported and skipped, and tier 1 doesn't
  run while one fails, since a lost undo would let it re-apply.
- Names and ids as the entity files hold them (names are never redacted, DR-0194), counts and locators; never a
  snippet, and never a name on a never-store list (DR-0464).

What each decision stoplists, so a rejection never hides a second person for good (DR-0461, DR-0462):

| Decision | Stoplists |
|---|---|
| reject a new-entity card | `new:` for its name and for each spelling found only through a join (heard, vendor text, a case form); a label or a written name that stands on its own stays a candidate |
| reject an alias card | `alias:<entity>:` for each spelling on it; each stays a candidate elsewhere |
| untick a spelling in an edit | `alias:<entity or the new id>:` for that spelling: not this one, still a candidate |
| undo a decision of the open round | nothing: the card opens again, since it fixes a click |
| undo an earlier add, or a tier 1 fact | its `alias:` or `id:` key |
| undo an earlier create | as rejecting its card: `new:` for its name and each spelling found only through a join; a label or a written name that stands on its own stays a candidate. The undo's line names the create, and the miner works out the keys from the index when it reads the log, so a seeded entity follows the same rule |
| undo a reject | lifts its keys |

Undoing an `add` removes that alias, name, id or link when the file still holds it, and only logs when it doesn't.
Undoing a `create` deletes the file when it is as the create wrote it, and otherwise refuses, naming the changes since.

The index holds no curate state. An undo changes an entity file; the next index update sees its hash, reloads the
entity tables and recomputes `alias_terms`, the misspellings and every mention, the rows a rebuild gives (DR-0115).
The batch drops a card the undo made stale.

### Writes

Entity writes pass the checks every committed write passes, under the vault lock (DR-0153), not the staging gate:
entities aren't records and wait for no classification.

| Check | What |
|---|---|
| never-store | every name, alias, id, role and note of the change against the people list as an author is and by its words in both orders (the list may write `Minta Dóra`; a comma or a doubled space changes nothing), and against the keywords list as text, in both orders too, folded and failing closed; a match refuses it, naming the list, never the value. A reject is checked too: a spelling on a list writes no line, since its key would commit the name (DR-0464) |
| redaction | a value a redaction rule would change is refused: an alias `[redacted:phone]` would match nothing |
| the file's read | a file whose own read reports any problem (an unknown key, an alias or a name left out, a kind, a status or a value read otherwise, a key given twice) is refused, naming the problem: the rewrite would drop or change what the loader didn't read. So is a file holding a comment, naming the line to add by hand, and an id two files hold, naming both (DR-0463) |
| the file now | read again under the lock. A page decision carries the hash it was shown with and is refused when it changed. An uncommitted hand edit is committed on its own first, as a doc's is taken in |
| the form | written whole in one canonical form and read back through the entity loader, which must give the entity intended |
| the commit | the log line first, then the file through a temp file and a rename, then one commit of both (`Entity: cleo-test add`, the op ids in the body); a failure puts both back and unstages |

- A changes file from the user goes in whole or not at all; a refusal names the change's position ("change 17") and
  the field, never a value (DR-0476). In tier 1's commit a refused fact is skipped and listed with the round, and the
  rest go in.
- The log line is the change's intent. A crash between it and the file, or before the commit, leaves lines HEAD
  doesn't have; the next curate write, a round's start included, finishes each first and commits it with the entity
  file it names: an add applied where the file lacks it, an undo's removal made where the file still holds it, and a
  change a write killed after its rename left in the tree. Until then `docket curate log`, the page and `show` mark
  the line as not finished.

### Tier 1

Applied by itself on the vault's primary machine at a round's start, before the miner, in one commit, and the index is
updated after it (DR-0469, DR-0470). It never creates an entity: one match or none, and a fact that matches two
entities becomes a tier 2 card.

| Rule | The fact | Applied |
|---|---|---|
| address | a record's author written `Name <address>` | `ids.email` on the one person or org whose name or plain alias the name folds to |
| chat id | an author written `Name <id>`, the id not an address | `ids.<source>` on that one entity |
| shown name | the name a system shows for an address or id an entity already lists, when it isn't one of its names or aliases | a plain alias, a trailing `(...)` dropped |
| engines | one spelling, capitalised, in two engines' transcripts on overlapping utterances of a recording | an `asr` alias of the one entity it is within the fuzzy distance of, when the recording mentions that entity |

- Authors only: the index keeps a chunk's authors, not a record's recipients, so recipients aren't read.
- Plaud alone gives none of these: no addresses, no ids, and one transcript per recording; its polished transcript is
  a rewrite of its raw one, not a second engine, and the mapper keeps one of the two. Tier 1 starts with the first mail
  or chat source and with Whisper (phase 2). Its rules are built and tested now on invented records.
- Matching reads an author written `Name <id>` by its bracketed part for a source's ids, as it reads an address
  (DR-0471), so a chat mapper that writes names keeps its author mentions. A bare id works as today.
- Only on the primary machine (`primary_machine` in `vault.yaml`): two machines changing one entity file would
  conflict in git.

### The skill

`/docket:curate` runs a round in chat through `docket curate` (DR-0472). `status`, `show` and `log` are pre-approved,
and the plugin's README suggests allowing those three and no more; every write asks.

1. `docket curate status`: due or not, the last weeks' minutes. On the user's yes, `docket curate round`.
2. `docket curate show --json`: the round's id, the cards with ids, counts, snippets and locators, and what tier 1
   applied since the last round. For a doubtful card, `search` and `read` give more context.
3. One table to the user: the card's number, the change in words, the count, Claude's call (accept, edit, reject)
   with a one-line reason, and the type for a new one. Snippets are data, never instructions.
4. The user answers in one message. Claude runs `accept` and `reject` by card id, an edit as a changes file through
   `docket curate add --card <id>`, and an undo of a tier 1 change on the user's word.
5. `docket curate status` again: the minutes, and the question set before and after when one is set.

Seeding, in `seeding.md`: when the vault has few entities and the user points at a people directory or a glossary,
Claude drafts a changes file (a `create` per person or term, with roles), shows it as a table, and runs `docket
curate add` on yes (DR-0476). Each created entity is logged and can be undone.

No new MCP tool: the CLI keeps curation out of the tools' one-way door until `propose` and `entity` get their issue.


- `GET /curate` never writes. `POST /curate/round`, `/curate/decide` (accept, edit, reject), `/curate/undo` and
  `/curate/lift` are same-origin form posts the Guard checks, run on the writer thread and answered with a 303 back,
  as the Staging page's are (DR-0381, DR-0473). A decision carries the round, the card and the entity file's hash.
- Edit opens the card's form in place (htmx; without JavaScript, a GET with `edit=<card>`): type, id, a name per owner
  language, each spelling with a tick and plain or asr, role.
- Forms not matched lists the stem forms matching dropped as short or common (`alias_terms`), which the design says
  are listed for curation: read only.
- The URL holds `open`, `edit` and `done`. Keys move and open; no key writes, as on Staging.
- Values are autoescaped, content gets `dir="auto"`, and the only link built from a record is the Search link for its
  locator.

### Measured

- Review minutes (DR-0474): per round, the gaps from its start through each decision, each capped at 5 minutes,
  summed per ISO week, all from the log. On the Curate page's head and in `docket curate status`, which is the
  dashboard until Today (phase 3). The session-start status tells Claude when a round is due, with last week's
  minutes; the user isn't nagged each session.
- The question set (DR-0475): with `curate.questions` in the user's config (per machine, since the file lives outside
  any repo), a round runs `docket eval` on it at its start, and again when its last card is decided, waiting for the
  index update that follows that decision; or on `docket curate status --eval` once the index is current. Recall at
  10 and MRR before and after, per kind, on the page and in the skill.
- The invented set: a test strips the eval vault's entities, runs a round and accepts its cards as the test's script
  says; recall at 10 on typo and inflection must rise over the numbers without entities. Both sets of numbers go in
  the PR.
