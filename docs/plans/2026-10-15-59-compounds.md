# Find hyphenated, joined and spaced compounds alike in search

## Goal

`kickoff`, `kick-off` and `kick off` find each other, and so do `follow-up`, `followup` and `follow up`, `e-mail` and
`email`, `on-line` and `online`, in English and Hungarian. Today FTS5 reads `kick-off` as two words and `kickoff` as
one, so `docket search "orbex kickoff"` finds only the word orbex in "Orbex kick-off with Dana".

| The text says | `kickoff` finds it | `kick-off` finds it | `kick off` finds it |
|---|---|---|---|
| kickoff | today | after this | after this |
| kick-off | after this | today | today |
| kick off | after this | today | today |

A hyphenated query word is read as its parts today, so `kick-off` and `kick off` already find each other; the joined
spelling is what's missing, both ways.

- In scope: hyphenated compounds indexed joined as well; at query time, a word also as two words in a row and two words
  in a row also as one; the word check and titles' leads (#57) reading compounds the way the index does; snippets and
  marks; the index's schema version, so it rebuilds; the tests, the perf test's shapes, the docs.
- Out of scope: a quoted phrase keeps its words as written (`"kickoff meeting"` doesn't find "kick-off meeting");
  three spaced words as one (`state of the art`); a word split into more than two; the parts of a joined compound
  (`millió` still doesn't find `tízmillió`, as #7 decided); a compound written apart after a word of one or two letters
  (`email` doesn't find "e mail", nor `online` "on line").

## Design

The index gains the joined form of each hyphenated compound; everything about spaced compounds happens at query time,
from the index's vocabulary. DR-0114 (columns per language), DR-0117 (ranking) and #57's stages stay as they are.

| Module | Change |
|---|---|
| `index/text.py` | `words_and_compounds(text)`: one pass giving a text's words and its hyphenated compounds joined; `Analyzer.index_values` appends the joined words |
| `index/schema.py` | `SCHEMA_VERSION` 2 |
| `index/query.py` | `word_groups` adds each word's joined words and splits; a chunk holding a word only through a split goes after the rest of its stage; the snippet's start |
| `index/terms.py` | the word check reads a text's compounds and a word's joined words and splits, scans for a split only in a text holding a term of each half, and says which words a text holds only through one |
| `web/templating.py` | `marked` marks two words in a row whose joined form is a query word: a hyphenated pair always, a spaced one when its halves have three and two letters |
| `tests/docket_testkit.py` | `build_compound_vault(root)`; `stem_every_word` reads compounds the slow way |

### Hyphenated compounds, indexed joined

A run of words joined by hyphens with no space between, one of them holding a letter, is also indexed joined:
`kick-off` adds `kickoff`, `e-mailt` adds `emailt`, `covid-19` adds `covid19`; `2026-10-12` adds nothing.

- A run of two or three words adds each joined stretch of two or more: `e-mail-cím` adds `email`, `mailcím` and
  `emailcím`, so `email` finds the Hungarian spelling of an e-mail address. A longer run (`state-of-the-art`, an id)
  adds only its whole.
- Hyphens are `-`, U+2010 and U+2011. A dash with spaces, an en dash or two hyphens join nothing.
- The joined words go after the chunk's own words in every column: as written in `folded`, stemmed by each language's
  stemmer in the others, so `e-mailben` and `e-mailt` hold `email` in `stem_hu`, and `kick-offs` holds `kickoff` in
  `stem_en`.
- After, not in place: the chunk's words keep their positions, so phrases and aliases match as before. Only the FTS5
  row changes. The `chunks` row (the text, its anchors, locator and hash) is as it was, so snippets, `read` and
  `verify` show the text as written.
- One pass splits the text into words and what lies between them, and a single hyphen between two words joins them.
  It replaces `words()` in `index_values` for 0.3 s more per 100,000 chunks; a regex for compounds took 4.4 s, five
  times the words' own pass. An update's dropped chunk recomputes the same values, as now.

Hungarian writes most compounds joined (`határidő`), and those already work. Hyphens come with loanwords (`e-mail`,
`on-line`), a suffix after one (`e-mailben`), and compounds of many syllables (`labdarúgó-világbajnokság`); each is now
found joined as well. A transcript that writes a compound apart (`határ idő`) is found by a split, below.

| Considered | Why not |
|---|---|
| the joined word in place of its parts | `kick` and `mail` would no longer find `kick-off` and `e-mail` |
| the joined word between the parts | every later word moves, so `"kick-off with dana"` breaks |
| a tokenizer that puts the joined word at the parts' position | FTS5 takes one only through its C API, which Python's `sqlite3` doesn't reach |
| a column of joined words | one more per language, and BM25 would weigh it apart from the words |

### Spaced compounds, at query time

Whether "kick off" is a compound depends on the whole vault, so search reads it from the index's vocabulary when
someone searches, rather than writing it into every chunk.

| The query has | It also matches | When |
|---|---|---|
| a word, `kickoff` | two words in a row, `kick off`, split where both halves are words the index holds, as written or through a stem | the first half has three letters or more and the second two, each with a letter; not both halves in half the chunks or more |
| two words in a row, `kick off` | their joined word, `kickoff`: each of the two also matches it | the index holds it |
| a hyphenated word, `kick-off` | its parts, as today, each also matching the joined stretches it is in, as the index joins them | the index holds them |

- A split matches as a phrase in `folded` and in each stemmed column, as a quoted phrase does: `kickoffs` splits into
  `kick offs`, whose English stems meet "kick off" and "kicked off".
- Two words that each also match their joined word read `(kick AND off) OR kickoff` in the all-words stage and
  `kick OR off OR kickoff` in the any-word stage, so #57's stages and term counts don't change.
- Only forms the index holds enter the expression, as now (DR-0230). A word stays in the query however its other
  spellings fare; only a split can be left out.
- The halves' lengths: a first half of two letters is a contraction's or a function word's (`we're` for `were`,
  `we'll` for `well`, `in to` for `into`), or a time's (`1000` would meet every 10:00); a second half of two is `up`,
  `in` or `on` (`follow up`, `log in`, `add on`).
- A letter in each half: numbers never split (`12345` would meet "123.45").
- Not both halves common: a phrase of two words in most chunks costs BM25 a pass over most chunks (187 ms against 47 ms
  below), and such pairs are function words (`may be`, `can not`).
- A split can find another word: "edit or delete" for `editor`, "form at" for `format`, "Harris on" for `Harrison`.
  In a probe of 40 rows saying "editor" and 3 saying "edit or delete", the three ranked first. So within each stage, a
  chunk holding a query word only through a split goes after the chunks holding it through its forms or joined words,
  and a title's lead doesn't count a word it holds only through a split (the word check, below).
- A word whose forms, joined words and splits the index holds none of goes into `absent`, as now. Built, after code
  review: a word none of whose forms the index holds goes there too unless a chunk says one of its splits, one
  `MATCH ... LIMIT 1` each, since both halves being words doesn't make the phrase one (`listcrane`).

Measured on the perf test's 100,000 chunks with 2% of the words hyphenated as random pairs, so nearly every compound is
new; and on 256,000 words of real English prose, the standard library's docstrings cut into 60-word chunks:

| Spaced compounds found by | Perf index | Its rebuild | Real prose |
|---|---|---|---|
| nothing: today, on the same text | 185.1 MB | 14-15 s | 1.1% of its words are hyphenated |
| a split or a join at query time, hyphenated ones indexed joined (this plan) | 188.7 MB | 28-29 s | - |
| every pair of words in a row, indexed joined | 313.6 MB | 136 s | 9.8 distinct pairs per word of its vocabulary |
| pairs whose joined form is a word elsewhere, indexed joined | 210.8 MB | 59 s | 4.5% of pairs, most often "a string", "a list", "may be", "e g" |
| pairs whose hyphenated form is somewhere, indexed joined | 210.8 MB | 39 s | 2.0% of pairs; chains add "end of", "up to", "value of" |

- This plan's rebuild cost is stemming each new joined word once: with 1% of the words hyphenated from 2,000
  compounds, repeating as prose repeats them, the index grew from 184.6 to 185.9 MB, and two rebuilds took 15.8 and
  16.4 s against 13.0 and 16.2 s today: within the noise.
- The synthetic words, made of syllables, join into one another far more often than prose's do, so the last two rows
  overstate their size; the prose column is their real share.
- Indexing spaced pairs also needs the whole vault before the first chunk is written: two passes per rebuild, and an
  update that meets a new `follow-up` re-indexing every older chunk that says "follow up", and again when the last one
  goes, to stay equal to a rebuild (DR-0115). At query time it costs a vocabulary lookup per split, for the words
  someone searches.

Query speed on the perf index with the compounds, best of three on the maintainer's desktop, with a prototype of the
query side that split at two letters on both halves, so these are upper bounds:

| Query | Time |
|---|---|
| the perf test's shapes (13 of its 15: this index had no entities) | at most 203 ms; main's 15 here: at most 234 ms |
| a hyphenated word; with a rare word | 15 ms; 62 ms |
| two words whose joined word is in 99.6% of the chunks | 218 ms (that word alone: 140 ms) |
| 12 words in six pairs, each joining into one of the 3,000 most common words | 219 ms |
| a word split into words in 59% and 34% of the chunks | 47 ms |
| three such words and a rare one | 110 ms |
| a word split into words in 99% and 94% of the chunks | 47 ms with the split left out; 187 ms with it |

### The word check, titles and snippets

The word check (#57) reads a text the way the index does, so a title's lead and the order inside a stage count a
compound as search matches it, and tell a word held only through a split from the word itself.

- A text's words are its words and its hyphenated compounds joined, from `words_and_compounds`; a joined word sits at
  its parts' positions for the lead's share.
- A query word is held through its forms, its joined words' forms, or one of its splits as a phrase; the check says
  which words a text holds only through a split.
- Within each stage, the chunks holding a query word only through a split go after the rest, lifted ones included,
  each part keeping its order; a title's lead doesn't count a word it holds only through a split. So `editor` ranks
  "editor" above "edit or delete", and a title "Edit or delete the crane list" lifts nothing for it. When the query
  has a split, the check runs on the all-words stage's candidates too.
- Built, after code review: in the any-word stage the rule is a tie-break, not a partition. A split-only chunk goes
  after the chunks holding as many terms and above those holding fewer; the partition stays in the all-words and
  prefix stages. As first built, a chunk holding two of three words through a split fell below every chunk holding
  one.
- A split is scanned for only in a text holding a term of each half, from the terms the first-letter screen already
  computes; any other text costs one set test. 36% of words of five letters or more admit a split, and most Hungarian
  inflected nouns do, since the index holds suffix tokens (`en`, `ben`, `kor`, `ig`: "kedd en", "projekt ben"). On 200
  candidates of 350 words, English and Hungarian, six query words each with a split took 556 ms scanning every text and
  129 ms gated, against 13 ms with no split (measured in review).
- Built: on the perf test's syllable words the same load took 375 ms with a cold stem cache and 172 ms warm, against
  250 and 78 ms with no split; 143 texts of the 1,200 were scanned. The synthetic words start with few letters, so the
  screen lets nearly every word through and the cold cache costs as much with no split at all. The test asserts the
  scan count always and the warm pass under one second without a tracer, and prints the cold one; a split's own cost,
  about 95 ms here, is the same cold or warm.
- So the lead "Orbex kick-off with Dana" holds every word of `orbex kickoff dana`, `orbex kick-off dana` and
  `orbex kick off dana`, and lifts its group for each.
- The first-letter screen takes the joined words' and the splits' first letters as well.
- A test holds the check to FTS5 itself: on the compound vault and on a sample of the perf index, for each query, the
  chunks the check says hold every word are exactly the all-words stage's.

Snippets are cut from `chunks.text`, which doesn't change, so they show "kick-off" or "kick off" as written.

- A hit's snippet, which the CLI and the MCP tool show, starts at the first query word, or at the first of two words
  in a row that are a hyphenated pair joining into one or one of its admitted splits, so a snippet for `were` never
  starts at "we're".
- The Search page can't see the vocabulary. It marks a query word as now, both words of a hyphenated pair whose joined
  form is one, and both of a spaced pair whose joined form is one when its halves have three and two letters: "kick
  off" and "kick-off" for `kickoff`, not "we're" for `were`.

## Doors and data

None of AGENTS.md's one-way doors. The MCP tools keep their names, parameters and result fields (DR-0150);
`QueryReading.words` still lists a hyphenated word's parts. Nothing in the vault is written.

The index's rows change, so `SCHEMA_VERSION` goes from 1 to 2 and an existing index rebuilds once from the vault, as
DR-0115 has it for any other schema:

| Where | After the upgrade |
|---|---|
| `docket search` | its update finds schema 1, rebuilds, then searches |
| the service | its update at start rebuilds; a search meanwhile waits 30 s, then says "the index is being built; try again in a minute" |
| a session's start | the status hook says "index: it has schema 1, and this Docket writes 2; the next search rebuilds it" |
| `docket search --no-update`, `docket index status` | say to run `docket index rebuild` |

A year's vault takes minutes; each machine rebuilds its own. The CHANGELOG line says so.

   equal `words()`), `test_index_build.py` (the FTS5 row holds the joined words, the `chunks` row doesn't change),
   `test_index_update.py` (adding and dropping hyphenated chunks equals a rebuild), `test_index_schema.py` (a schema 1
   index is stale, and `update` rebuilds it).
2. `build_compound_vault(root)` in `docket_testkit.py`, built once per session through `template_with`. Test:
   `test_the_compound_vault_holds_each_spelling`.
3. The query: hyphenated words' joined stretches, pairs' joined words and splits in `word_groups`; `absent`. Tests:
   the search rows below.
4. The word check: compounds, the split gate, split-only chunks after the rest, leads without them. Tests: the title,
   order and agreement rows, the extended equivalence test.
5. Snippets and marks. Tests: the snippet row, and `test_web_search`'s marks.
6. The perf test: compounds in its vault, five shapes, the word check's time and the agreement on a sample. Tests:
   `test_queries_answer_in_well_under_a_second` and the two below.
7. The docs below, then `finish-work`: `uv run gate full`, a draft PR, `review-code` until it is clean.

## Risks

| Risk | Status |
|---|---|
| A split finds another word (`editor` finds "edit or delete", `format` "form at", `Harrison` "Harris on"): 6% of real prose's words of four letters or more have a split the prose says, most of them real compounds (`filename`, `callback`, `setup`) | resolved: such a chunk goes after those holding the word itself in each stage, and a lead doesn't count the word; it is still found, and only for a word someone searches |
| The word check scans long candidates for splits, and most Hungarian inflected nouns admit one | resolved: a split is scanned for only in a text holding a term of each half, 129 ms against 556 ms for six splits; the perf test times it |
| A compound whose halves are both in half the chunks or more isn't found when written apart (`set up` where both words are everywhere) | accepted: its joined and hyphenated spellings still are, and the split would cost a pass over most chunks |
| Rebuilds take longer with many distinct hyphenated compounds, each stemmed once | accepted: 15 s more per 100,000 chunks when 2% of the words are new compounds; within the noise when they repeat as prose does |
| A joined word appended after the chunk's last word makes a phrase with it (`dana kickoff`) | accepted: only a quoted phrase of exactly that pair meets it |
| `asr` aliases walk a vocabulary holding the joined words, so a misspelling may match one | accepted: mentions only boost, and `alias_terms` lists what they matched |
| The one-second limits on CI's Windows runner, about four times slower | accepted: the slowest new shape costs what a common word alone does, which CI already runs; `gate os` shows it |
| Every index rebuilds once after the upgrade, and the service's first searches wait | accepted: minutes, said by the CHANGELOG and the session-start hook |
| Snowball leaves some joined words unstemmed (`pdfben` stays `pdfben` in Hungarian) | accepted: the parts are still words of their own, so `pdf` finds `PDF-ben` as today |
