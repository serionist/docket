# Add meetings, talking points and streams to the ledger

Meetings and streams join the items' op files as objects made by an add of their kind; talking points are ops on their
meeting. A meeting is keyed by its calendar event, a stream by its name, and a keyed add may continue a meeting that had
none. Rollover and archive are ops the writer writes, so the fold never reads the clock. Two PRs: ops, then the index.

## Goal

- Done: every field todo.py keeps on a meeting, a point and a stream is kept as ops, and an item names its meeting.
  Points open, answered, dropped, rolled or proposed, with `from`/`to`; rollover and archive as ops; `suggest`, a read.
- Out: meetings from calendar records and links by time (#200); the `ledger` tool (#201); the import (#202) and the
  owner's export script, outside Docket (roadmap step 15); the pages (#203 to #206); STATUS.md (#208).

## Design

### Objects

| Add | Makes | Key | Number |
|---|---|---|---|
| `add` | an item (DR-0611) | - | A- (DR-0613) |
| `meeting` | a meeting | `event`, when it has one | M-, its own sequence, the same rule |
| `stream` | a stream | `name` | - |

- Only these make objects, each with `item` equal to its own id; every other op names its object by `item`. An op
  naming no add, or another known kind with `item` equal to its id, is a problem. An unknown kind's add and its ops are
  kept, claim no number and change nothing. PR 1 changes #198's `fold_item`, which finds adds by `op == "add"`.
- One key, one object (DR-0105): its adds fold in id order, each later one as a `set` of its fields at its place.
- A keyed `meeting` add may carry `continues`, an unkeyed meeting's id: that meeting joins the key's object, its id
  and number the object's. The writer writes one, key held or not, when a record matches an unkeyed meeting by time and
  subject (#200, as todo.py's upsert did) or the owner joins two on the Meetings page. One key continues one meeting
  and one meeting is continued by one key: the first such add by id wins; a later `continues` is left out and named.
- Otherwise the object's id and number are its first add's. A later add's number finds the object only while no other
  object holds it, so it never takes one or causes a renumber. Keys and `continues` are set by adds only, so which ops
  are one object is known before anything folds. Undoing a meeting's add undoes every add of its object in one write.
- The export script gives an old meeting the `event` and `series` of a calendar record matching its time and subject,
  its other fields as they were; one with no match stays keyless, for #200 to continue.

### The ops

| Op | On | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| `meeting` | - | `number`; `fields`: subject, start, end; event, continues, series, organiser, where, response, attendees, stream, notes, outcome |
| `stream` | - | `fields`: name, status; where, next, deadline |
| `set` | a meeting or a stream | `fields`, any of its add's but the key and `continues`; `null` clears an optional one |
| `point` | a meeting | `fields`: text; item, from, proposed. Its id is the point's |
| `point_set` | a meeting | `point`, the point's id; `fields`, any of text, state, answer, item, to, proposed |
| `material` | a meeting | as an item's: a recording, a chat or a mail it links (todo.py's `recordings` and `links`) |
| `archive` | a meeting | -; it needs no open point, and only its undo brings the meeting back |
| `log`, `undo`, `renumber` | any object | as an item's (DR-0614) |

- `event`: DR-0105's key, the event's own id plus an occurrence's original start, as text; #200 and the export script
  build it alike. `series`: the series' id. `start`, `end`: `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM±HH:MM`, the local time with its offset,
  so DST and a zone change stay exact (todo.py's times take the zone's offset then; its `HH:MM` end lands on the
  start's day or the next). `response`: accepted, tentative, declined, none, organiser, cancelled. `attendees`: a list
  of names. `stream`, `name`: slugs. `deadline`: a day. `proposed`: true. The rest is text, redacted (DR-0627).
  Kinds, states, responses and statuses read as text and are checked when written; field rules are per object.
- An item gains `meeting`, a meeting's id, in `add` and `set`; the writer takes an M- number and writes the id.

### Points

- A point's id is its `point` op's id; its `n` is its place among the meeting's standing points by that id, todo.py's
  list order. `M-0040#2` is shown, never stored.
- One op per change; the writer writes what todo.py infers (an answer to an open point sets `state: answered`, and an
  answer or a keep clears `proposed`). Keeping a meeting's proposals is a `point_set` each. Removing a point is an undo
  of its `point`; dropping one keeps it. A rolled point changes only by an undo; any other `point_set` on it is idle.
- `from` names the point a copy came from, `to` the copy. A copy stands only while its source stands and the source's
  winning (first) roll names it; the fold settles copies along their `from` chains before it counts open points. The
  export script pairs them in todo.py's order; one it can't pair names the meeting, which the index tells apart.
- A point to an item (todo.py's `point item`) is one write: an item `add` with `meeting`, then a `point_set` of it.

### Rollover and archive

- A roll is two ops in one write: a `point` on the next meeting with `from`, then a `point_set` on the old point,
  `state: rolled`, `to` the copy. Its undo undoes both, refused while the copy has rolled on. DR-0614's waking undo
  counts only rolls and copies here, so undoing a roll never undoes another clone's answer; that answer then applies.
- The writer's `rollover` change finds what is due as todo.py does: a cancelled meeting at once, one that is over with
  an outcome, any other 24 hours after its end. The sync asks for it (#210); nothing rolls on every write any more.
- The next meeting is the next by start, not cancelled or declined, with the same `series`, else todo.py's
  `next_in_series` by subject and organiser. The export script and #200's updates write `series` from the matched
  record, so the subject decides only where no record matched. A point with nowhere to go stays open; the day lists it.
- The sync archives a meeting started 14 days back or more with no open point, skipping one whose archive was undone.
  A `set` on an archived meeting is idle and the writer writes nothing for it, refusing nothing, so the sync's write
  never fails on an old meeting (#200 relies on it). Other changes to one are refused but an undo.

### Streams

- A stream is made once per name: `status` active, waiting or closed, `where`, `next`, `deadline`; `set` changes them,
  and `updated` is the day of the latest change. The writer refuses an item slug no stream holds, once one exists.
- #208 renders those six at a path `vault.yaml` holds; the export script makes one stream per todo.py stream.

### The fold, and the index (PR 2)

- `fold.py` groups lines by object, key and `continues`, folds each by kind, then settles copies. `vault check` also
  names a `point`, `from`, `to` or `continues` naming nothing it can take, an idle copy and an M- clash.

| Table | What |
|---|---|
| `meetings` | each meeting: number, event, series, start, end, day, subject, organiser, where, response, attendees, stream, notes, outcome, materials, archived, the add's by, its last op |
| `points` | each point: id, meeting, n, text, state, answer, item, from, to, proposed, its `point` op's day and by |
| `meeting_log`, `stream_log` | every op of a meeting (its points' too) or of a stream, with `item_log`'s columns |
| `meeting_numbers` | each M- number and the meetings that held it, as `item_numbers` |
| `streams` | each stream: name, status, where, next, deadline, updated, its last op |

- `items` gains `meeting`. An update reads a grown file as DR-0616 does and folds again each object its lines name, the
  objects of their keys and `continues`, the meetings their `from` and `to` name, and each number's holders. Schema 12.
- `suggest(meeting)`, todo.py's rules: open items linked to it (not prep), then replies, waits and decisions on someone
  in it, in its stream, then waits in its stream; none already on a point; 15 at most. #201 and #205 call it.

## Doors and data

One-way, PR 1's title naming them: adds and object kinds (DR-0620), keys and `continues` (DR-0621), the meeting's op,
fields and M- numbers (DR-0622), points (DR-0623), streams (DR-0625), an item's `meeting` (DR-0626); AGENTS.md's ledger
line gains them. `medium`: the text rules (DR-0627), rollover and archive (DR-0624), the index (DR-0628); `suggest` is
cheap (DR-0629). Data: none; nothing writes ops before #201 and #202, so no #198-only Docket meets a meeting's op.

## Tests

- PR 1: a golden line per new op; two clones adding one week, numbering shifted by one: one meeting per event, no
  renumber; a continued meeting keeps its id and number; one point rolled on two clones, to one meeting or two: one
  copy; a roll undone whole, refused once its copy rolled on, keeping another clone's answer; a set on an archived
  meeting; a start across DST; a rollover folded a month later; an unknown kind; #198's tests unchanged; perf with
  2,000 meetings and 20,000 point ops added, under a second. Invented people and subjects only.
- PR 2: rebuild equals update for a grown file, a key two clones added, a continued meeting and an M- clash; `suggest`.
