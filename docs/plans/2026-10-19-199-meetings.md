# Add meetings, talking points and streams to the ledger

Meetings and streams go into the items' op files as objects of their own: an op whose `item` is its own id makes one,
and its kind says what. A meeting's talking points are ops on the meeting. A meeting is keyed by its calendar event and
a stream by its name, and the first add of a key holds it. Rollover and archive are ops the writer writes, so the fold
never reads the clock. Two PRs: the ops, the fold and the writer, then the index.

## Goal

- Done: every field todo.py keeps on a meeting, a point and a stream is kept as ops, and an item names its meeting.
  Points open, answered, dropped, rolled or proposed, with `from`/`to`; rollover and archive as ops; `suggest`, a read.
- Out: meetings from calendar records and links by time (#200); the `ledger` tool (#201); the import and the export
  (#202, #215); the pages (#203 to #206); STATUS.md and its path (#208). #198's door stays as it is.

## Design

### Objects

| Add | Makes | Key | Number |
|---|---|---|---|
| `add` | an item (DR-0611) | - | A- (DR-0613) |
| `meeting` | a meeting | `event`, when it has one | M-, its own sequence, the same rule |
| `stream` | a stream | `name` | - |

- An object's add is the op whose `item` is its own id; every other op names its object by `item`. An op naming an id
  no add made is a problem, as now. One naming an object whose add is of a kind this Docket doesn't know, and that
  add, are kept, change nothing and are no problem: a later object joins the same way.
- One key, one object (DR-0105). The first add of a key by id is the object's id; each later one folds in as a `set`
  of its fields at its place, and its number still finds the object (`M-0041` names the meeting). Ops naming any of
  them are the object's. An undone add is left out; the object stands while one of its adds does.
- A key is set by the add only, so which ops are one object is known before anything folds. The writer never adds a
  key the ledger holds; a second add comes only from two clones apart. A meeting with no `event` is its own. The
  export (#215) gives an old meeting the `event` of a calendar record matching its time and subject, its fields kept.

### The ops

| Op | On | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| `meeting` | - | `number`; `fields`: subject, start, end; event, series, organiser, where, response, attendees, stream, notes, outcome |
| `stream` | - | `fields`: name, status; where, next, deadline |
| `set` | a meeting or a stream | `fields`, any of its add's but the key; `null` clears an optional one |
| `point` | a meeting | `fields`: text; item, from, proposed. Its id is the point's |
| `point_set` | a meeting | `point`, the point's id; `fields`, any of text, state, answer, item, to, proposed |
| `material` | a meeting | as an item's: a recording, a chat or a mail it links (todo.py's `recordings` and `links`) |
| `archive` | a meeting | -; it needs no open point, and only its undo brings the meeting back |
| `log`, `undo`, `renumber` | any object | as an item's (DR-0614) |

- `start` and `end`: local times, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM` in `vault.yaml`'s zone, as `day` is (todo.py's `HH:MM` end lands
  on the start's day or the next). `response`: accepted, tentative, declined, none, organiser, cancelled. `attendees`: a
  list of names. `event`, `series`: the calendar's keys, opaque text that #200 sets. `stream`, `name`: slugs.
  `deadline`: a day. `proposed`: true. The rest is text, redacted as DR-0618 has it; an organiser or attendee on the
  never-store people list refuses the write, as `who` does, and a match in `event`, `series` or a ref refuses it.
- An item gains `meeting`, a meeting's id, in `add` and `set`; the writer takes an M- number and writes the id.
- Kinds, states, responses and statuses read as text, checked when written; an item's field rules hold on its ops only.

### Points

- A point's id is its `point` op's id; its `n` is its place among the meeting's standing points by that id, todo.py's
  list order. `M-0040#2` is shown, never stored.
- One op per change; the writer writes what todo.py infers (an answer to an open point sets `state: answered`, and an
  answer or a keep clears `proposed`). Keeping a meeting's proposals is a `point_set` each. Removing a point is an undo
  of its `point`; dropping one keeps it. A rolled point changes only by an undo; any other `point_set` on it is idle.
- `from` names the point a copy came from, `to` the copy. The export pairs them in order, as todo.py's rollover appends
  them; one it can't pair names the meeting, which the index tells apart. The mirror writes each as an M- number.
- A point to an item is one write: an item `add` with `meeting` and source `meeting:<id>` (waiting on `Name` for a
  "Name: ..." text, else a task), then a `point_set` of `item`.

### Rollover and archive

- A roll is two ops in one write: a `point` on the next meeting with `from`, then a `point_set` on the old point,
  `state: rolled`, `to` the copy. The writer's undo of either undoes both. Two clones rolling one point leave one copy:
  a second copy of one source is idle, and on the source the first roll wins.
- The writer's `rollover` change finds what is due as todo.py does: a cancelled meeting at once, one that is over with
  an outcome, any other 24 hours after its end. The sync asks for it (#210); nothing rolls on every write any more.
- The next meeting is the next by start, not cancelled or declined: the same `series` when both have one, else the same
  subject (case and spaces folded), else one subject starting with the other, 15 characters or more, with the same
  organiser (todo.py's `next_in_series`). Before #200 no meeting has a `series`, so the subject decides. A point with
  nowhere to go stays open, and the day lists it.
- The sync archives a meeting that started 14 days back or more with no open point, and skips one whose archive was
  undone (as DR-0615). The writer refuses any change to an archived meeting but an undo.

### Streams

- A stream is made once per name, `status` active, waiting or closed, with `where`, `next` and `deadline`; `set`
  changes them, and `updated` is the day of the latest op that changed one. An item's `stream` stays a slug (DR-0611);
  once the ledger holds a stream, the writer refuses a slug none holds. The export makes one per todo.py stream.
- #208 renders name, status, where, next, deadline and updated, todo.py's frontmatter, at a path `vault.yaml` holds.

### The fold and the index (PR 2)

- `fold.py` finds the adds, groups each line under its object by key and folds each by kind, numbers per sequence.
  `vault check` also names a `point_set` naming no point, a `from` or `to` naming nothing, a stray copy, an M- clash.

| Table | What |
|---|---|
| `meetings` | each meeting: number, event, series, start, end, day, subject, organiser, where, response, attendees, stream, notes, outcome, materials, archived, the add's by, its last op |
| `points` | each point: id, meeting, n, text, state, answer, item, from, to, proposed, its `point` op's day and by |
| `meeting_log`, `stream_log` | every op of a meeting (its points' too) or of a stream, with `item_log`'s columns |
| `meeting_numbers` | each M- number and the meetings that held it, as `item_numbers` |
| `streams` | each stream: name, status, where, next, deadline, updated, its last op |

- `items` gains `meeting`. An update reads a grown file as DR-0616 does and folds again each object its new lines name,
  every object of their keys and the holders of each number they claim. Schema 12, after #198's 11.
- `suggest(meeting)`, todo.py's rules: open items linked to it (not prep), then replies, waits and decisions on someone
  in it, in its stream, then waits in its stream; none already on a point; 15 at most. #201 and #205 call it.

## Doors and data

Set for good, PR 1's title naming them, each `cost: one-way`: adds and object kinds (DR-0620); keys (DR-0621); the
meeting's op, fields, values and M- numbers (DR-0622); points (DR-0623); streams (DR-0625); an item's `meeting`
(DR-0626). The text rules are `medium` (DR-0627). Not doors: rollover and archive rules (DR-0624), the index tables
(DR-0628), `suggest` (DR-0629), `n`, the log's words. Data: none; nothing writes ops before #201 and #202, so no vault
meets a meeting's op in a #198-only Docket. AGENTS.md's ledger line gains DR-0620 to DR-0626.

## Tests

- PR 1: a golden line per new op; one event added on two clones is one meeting with both clones' points and numbers;
  one point rolled on two clones gives one copy; a roll undone whole; a rollover folds the same a month later; one
  stream from two clones; an unknown object kind is no problem; #198's tests unchanged; perf with 2,000 meetings and
  20,000 point ops added, still under a second. Invented people and subjects only.
- PR 2: rebuild equals update for a grown file, a key two clones added and an M- clash; `suggest` on todo.py's cases.

## Risks

- One point rolled to two meetings by two clones: the first roll wins, the stray copy stays open for the check. Rare.
- A change of the owner's zone moves every meeting's local times: accepted, as for `day`.
